\section{The experiment in one paragraph}
Train two robot policies that are \emph{identical} except one must also predict how
the object will move (a small ``prediction head'' bolted on during training, deleted
afterwards). Test both under physics they never saw (heavier, slipperier objects).
If the prediction-trained one degrades more slowly, run controlled follow-ups to find
out \emph{why}. Every claim is protected by pre-registered rules and go/no-go gates.

\textbf{Status:} the control task (T4) is complete; the primary task (T3) is
validated, its teacher certified, all 120 arms at the $10^3$/$10^4$ scales trained,
and the $10^3$ tier now fully evaluated at 10 seeds per arm. Its result is
two-sided and is reported as measured (Section~10): adding a prediction head does
not separate from the no-head baseline, but pointing that head at the \emph{true}
next state rather than a shuffled one does --- separably. A second task that pays
for physics knowledge (T8 stack-and-carry, from the user's design) now passes the
validity gate at $+17.2$ points. Four other task designs were measured invalid and
honestly descoped, each leaving a reusable design law behind.

\subsection*{Glossary}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{c} --- the hidden physics of an episode (mass, friction, off-center
weight). The robot feels c but is never told it.
\item \textbf{Teacher} --- a controller that acts \emph{with} c visible and
generates expert demonstrations; student policies imitate them blind. Originally
an RL agent; on T3 the certified teacher is now a physics-calibrated scripted
controller (Section~6) --- what matters is that its actions depend on the true c
and pass the same quality gates.
\item \textbf{$\delta$ grid} --- 210 physics settings outside the training range,
used to measure how fast a policy breaks as physics drift.
\item \textbf{Gate} --- a pre-planned go/no-go check. Skipping one silently
invalidates everything after it. Gates failing early and loudly is the design working.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Student policy} ($\sim$1.0M parameters, trained from scratch --- pretrained
weights would break the identical-data constraint):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Flow matching} (Diffusion-Policy style, rectified-flow objective):
the velocity head learns
$\;\mathcal{L}=\lVert v_\theta(a^\tau,\tau,\phi(h))-(a_1-a_0)\rVert^2$;
at test time 10 Euler steps turn noise into an 8-action chunk, of which the first
4 are executed before re-planning.
\item \textbf{Arm B adds only} $\lambda\lVert g_\theta(\phi(h),a)-x^{\text{obj}}_{t+1..t+H}\rVert^2$.
The head is deliberately \emph{weak} (2 layers): it can only succeed if the physics
information is already in $\phi$ --- a deep head could solve prediction internally
and teach the trunk nothing. It predicts the \emph{object's} state (13-d pose+velocity),
never the robot's joints (those are trivially predictable). Three target types:
one-step, multi-step ($H{=}8$), latent (stop-gradient). Verified: A and B are
byte-identical at deployment.
\item \textbf{Controls}: B-shuffled (targets randomly permuted --- one line) and
arm C (forecast concatenated to $\phi$ at inference, as published hybrids do).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Teacher} (PPO, stock script, only the observation changed): actor--critic,
each a 3$\times$256 tanh MLP; input $=$ student obs (41) $+$ c (4).

\subsection{The certified teacher is a calibrated script, not an RL agent}
The task-validation controller already beat the bar: a scripted flicker whose
launch speed and run-up length are looked up from the \emph{true} physics.
Recalibrating its lookup table at full grid resolution ($5\times4$ physics cells,
5 speeds $\times$ 4 run-ups, 128 episodes per cell) produced a teacher that passed
\textbf{both} certification gates: \textbf{mean success 0.634} (bar $\ge$0.55) and
\textbf{worst per-axis unevenness 0.072} (bar $\le$0.10) --- see
Fig.~\ref{fig:certflat}. Nothing about the experiment requires the teacher to be
RL: it must succeed broadly, evenly, and \emph{act on c}, which a calibration
table does explicitly and interpretably (slippery blocks get gentler launches;
grippy ones full speed with a long run-up). A side benefit: a scripted teacher
cannot reward-hack, which removes one class of anti-cheat geometry from future
task designs.

\section{The central finding: the escorting exploit and the tunnel}
A required gate: before the experiment runs, the plain policy must \emph{visibly
fail} under extreme physics, else there is no gap to explain. It refused to fail
(0.92 in-range $\to$ 0.80 at extremes; the continuous distance measure plateaued
too). Instrumentation found the cause: the gripper stayed within 6\,cm of the
object for $\sim$80\% of the final approach --- \textbf{escorting}. Continuous
contact $=$ continuous correction $=$ physics never matter.

\textbf{Fix: the tunnel} (visible in Fig.~1) --- a roof over the channel directly
before the slot; the object slides under, the hand cannot. The final stretch is now
a committed free slide whose stopping point c decides, while both recovery
directions survive. Verified: identical launches now spread outcomes across
\textbf{17\,cm}. Follow-on mechanics, found cheaply with the scripted expert:
launches need a \emph{run-up} (a strike from standstill enters at half speed and
dies under the roof at every strength); with a 13\,cm charge, one \emph{fixed}
flick reaches 43--46\% success.

Two more measured exploits completed the design. \textbf{Overshoot-escort}: a
deliberate overshoot could be walked back by contact, physics-free --- measured as
a c-aware flick beating a c-blind one by \emph{nothing}; fixed by a second roof
past the slot, so both failure directions are committed. \textbf{Command
saturation}: heavy/grippy blocks capped every controller at 19--22\% (the arm
could not launch them) --- fixed by a deliberately slick channel floor, restoring
authority while preserving the full relative spread of block friction.
\textbf{Final validation (3{,}000+ episodes)}: a controller told the true physics
succeeds \textbf{61.7\%} vs \textbf{45.0\%} for the same controller with one fixed
launch --- a $+16.7\pm3.6$-point knowledge premium. That gap is what the main
experiment asks policies to learn from demonstrations.

Two design laws emerged, both measured rather than assumed. (1)~\textbf{Both
failure directions must cost something}: wherever overshoot or a redo is free
(T1 dump, T2, pre-roof T3), one fixed blind action ties the physics-aware
ceiling. (2)~\textbf{Free correction erases knowledge}: T6 (built on the
established Push-T benchmark, hidden mass/friction/COM on the tee) tested this
four ways --- loose tolerance, widened COM, precision tolerance, and a
high-power precision rerun --- and the premium is zero every time, even on
time-to-success. A pusher that watches the object respond \emph{is} doing
system identification; explicit knowledge has nothing left to add. Combined
with T3's $+16.7$ under forced commitment, the boundary is sharp:
\textbf{the knowledge premium lives exactly where correction is impossible}.
Per the standing gate-before-investment rule, T6 gets no teacher or arms; the
boundary finding itself goes in the paper.

\section{First pre-registered result: the control task behaves \hfill complete}
All 60 T4 arms trained (A, B$\times$3 targets, B-shuffled, C $\times$ 10 seeds;
zero failures) and evaluated on the $\delta$ grid. \textbf{A-vs-B degradation
slopes are statistically identical on every tag} (e.g.\ composition
$+0.0113\pm0.0009$ vs $+0.0115\pm0.0014$; all CIs overlap): on a task where
physics cannot matter, the prediction head changes nothing --- the specificity
control passes, certifying the train$\to$evaluate$\to$slope pipeline at 10-seed
power. The probes add the mechanism: \textbf{B's trunk decodes twice the mass
information of A's} (ridge $R^2$ 0.53--0.59 vs 0.27--0.31) while the shuffled
control sits at A's level --- the prediction \emph{content} injects physics
knowledge into the network --- and friction/COM stay undecodable, physically
correct for a grasped object. Together: \emph{knowledge without effect}, exactly
what a control task should show.

\midrule

B-multistep       & $+0.0131 \pm 0.0019$ & CIs overlap $\to$ effect absent \\
B-latent          & $+0.0139 \pm 0.0023$ & CIs overlap $\to$ effect absent \\

Lower is better: the slope is how fast regret grows as the physics is shifted
away from training. Two things follow, and the second is the interesting one.

\textbf{(1) Adding a prediction head does not measurably help.} Every B
variant, and C, overlaps A. Under the falsification rule frozen before any
arm ran, the verdict at this scale is \textbf{effect absent}, and that is
what we report. All three B variants land below A by $\sim$12--17\%, so the
direction is consistent, but the intervals do not separate.

\textbf{(2) Predicting the \emph{right} next state beats predicting a wrong
one, and this does separate.} B-shuffled is the tightest control in the
design: identical architecture, parameter count, loss form and optimizer, and
the same auxiliary gradient magnitude --- the only difference is that its
prediction target comes from a different episode. Against it:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Contrast} & \textbf{all} & \textbf{composition} & \textbf{Verdict} \\
\midrule

So the auxiliary loss is not inert: pointed at the true next state it makes

arm in the table --- a wrong dynamics target is actively harmful relative to
no target at all. The ordering B $<$ B-latent $<$ C $<$ A $<$ B-shuffled is
what the hypothesis predicts; what is missing at this data scale is the
A-to-B step clearing the noise floor. That is precisely what the $10^4$ and
$10^5$ tiers are for: if the effect is real, its margin over A should grow
with data. Those evaluations are running.

\section{T8 ``stack-and-carry'': a second task that pays \hfill gate passing}
Task proposed by the user: stack objects of different shape and carry the
stack somewhere else. As stated it would have failed the gate the way T2 did
(a slow careful carry succeeds under every physics), so it was built in the
regime where the two design laws say a premium can exist. A tall block
($6\times6\times10$\,cm) whose centre of mass is hidden and offset by up to
$\pm1.2$\,cm must be placed on a narrow beam ($12\times3.6\times2.4$\,cm),
which is then grasped at its free end and carried 34\,cm inside a 140-step
budget. The block stands only while its hidden mass stays inside a band
around the beam's centre line, and the placement is committed the instant the
gripper opens.

\textbf{Gate: aware $0.86$ vs blind $0.69$, premium $+17.2$ points} at 64
episodes per arm --- healthy success far above the $0.55$ bar and a premium
edging T3's. The full 512-episode gate is running. Three iterations, each
leaving a measured law behind:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.15em]
\item \textbf{A monotone choice against a $c$-independent floor can never
pay.} The first build made the carry \emph{speed} the knowledge-bearing
choice. The probe found a real physics cliff --- at friction $0.33$ the block
sheared off above speed $0.5$, at $0.68$ even $0.9$ survived --- and the
premium is still provably zero: the slip threshold $s_{\text{safe}}(c)$ moves
with $c$ but the deadline floor $s_{\min}$ does not, and slower is always
safer, so a blind controller parks at $s_{\min}$ --- the same corner an
omniscient one would pick --- and succeeds on exactly the same episodes.
This sharpens law~(1): both failure directions must cost \emph{and} both
thresholds must move with $c$, which is exactly why T3's flick pays.
\item \textbf{The informed arm must be calibrated, not derived.} Rebuilt in
the balance regime, the ``obvious'' aware controller --- shift the block by
$-$COM so its mass sits on the beam centre line --- measured $0.28$ against
blind's $0.47$. A swept placement (Figure~\ref{fig:t8mech}) shows why:
success peaks when the mass sits $+1.0$ to $+1.5$\,cm toward the goal, not at
zero, because the carry's opening acceleration throws the block the other
way. An analytically correct aware controller made a good task read as a
failed gate --- the same lesson as T3's calibrated flick table, and a trap
worth stating in the paper.
\item With both arms calibrated over the same grid --- aware gets a
per-bucket (speed, bias) plus the COM correction, blind gets one calibrated
pair, which is the best a $c$-blind controller can do since the COM angle is
uniform --- the premium is $+17.2$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Measurement tools, validated the hard way}
Probes (small readouts measuring how much physics knowledge a policy's internals
hold) and ablations (deleting it) had to pass a gate on the calibration tier,
where ground truth is known. Five failures, each a real trap caught on a toy:
(1)~no excitation --- the expert settles the system and physics vanish from data;
(2)~shortcut learning --- imitation via a ratio trick, no physics represented;
(3)~an under-identified system --- the optimal gain barely varied with physics
(variance 0.05), nothing to decode; (4)~query-entangled features --- probe
directions did not transfer; (5)~\textbf{redundant encodings defeat linear
erasure} --- after deleting 16 directions the quantity stayed readable
(R$^2$ 0.60--0.82) and the deletion hurt no more than deleting random directions,
while the decoy control stayed exactly null. Final state: \pass{}, with a
pre-registered consequence: \textbf{ablation results count as evidence only when
positive}.

\section{Robustness practices in force}
\begin{itemize}
\item Arms differ by exactly one loss term; same data, architecture, parameter
count, optimizer, seeds. Students trained from scratch.
\item Pre-registration (\texttt{configs/preregistration.json}): falsification rule
(overlapping 95\% CIs on A-vs-B degradation slopes over $\ge$10 seeds $=$ no
effect, and that is the reported result), $\lambda$-sweep spec (20 values
$\times$ 5 seeds), substitution budgets (exactly 12 configs per rival mechanism,
closing criterion $|s_{\text{sub}}-s_{\text{treat}}| < 0.25\,|s_{\text{base}}-s_{\text{treat}}|$),
ablation interpretation rule --- all frozen before any arm runs; amendments dated.
\item Fixed evaluation seeds; interpolation / extrapolation / composition reported
separately, never averaged; binary success \emph{and} continuous distance logged
(success saturates and can hide differences).
\item Measured limitation: GPU physics repeats only to $\sim$4\,cm on violent
launches --- absorbed by many episodes per grid point.
\item Every gate output archived in \texttt{reports/}; every failure documented
(this report, \texttt{PROGRESS.md}); task/protocol changes only before arm launch.
\end{itemize}
